1Executive Summary

Core Concept

Leverage the Republic of Indonesia's successful precedent in issuing sovereign Panda Bonds — and its clearly stated willingness to re-issue — to encourage Indonesia's Ministry of Finance to launch a new Panda Bond issuance (RMB-denominated), with proceeds channeled through compliant mechanisms (government on-lending, sovereign fund investment, or dedicated budget allocation) into two high-return strategic projects: the PT Esa Jaya Labdagati nickel trading & mining project (~EUR 200M) and the Russindo offshore natural gas development project (~EUR 800M).

Total funding requirement of approximately EUR 1 billion (~RMB 7.8 billion) is of the same order of magnitude as Indonesia's inaugural Panda Bond issuance of RMB 7 billion — making this genuinely feasible.

3.2 Legal & Policy Basis for Re-Issuance

Explicit Statement by Indonesia's Finance Minister

Finance Minister Purbaya Yudhi Sadewa stated at the APBN KiTa press briefing on July 22, 2026:

"Because this is a new market, we will conduct a tentative initial issuance. Although demand may be very large, we have limited the first issuance to USD 1 billion. If needed, we can conduct a larger re-issuance within a few months."

The Minister further noted that Indonesia's total global bond issuance requirement for 2026 is approximately USD 3 billion, and if Chinese market conditions remain favorable, the government will maximize use of the Panda Bond instrument to cover a larger share of its financing needs.

Recommended Approach: Hybrid Structure (Channel D)

Nickel project (short-term high return): Prioritize government on-lending (Channel A) — with 11-month payback and strong cash flow, the project suits debt financing. Government raises RMB at ~2%, on-lends at 4–5%, capturing 200–300 bps of spread, with a clearly defined repayment source (OSS/Tsingshan offtake agreements).

Gas project (long-term large-scale): Prioritize Danantara equity investment (Channel B) — with NPV of USD 789M and IRR of 26.1%, the project suits equity participation sharing upside. Danantara has an established strategic direction in gas/energy infrastructure investment, and capital can be deployed against four-stage milestones to control risk.

Total allocation: Recommended Panda Bond re-issuance size of RMB 8–10 billion (~USD 1.1–1.4 billion), covering the combined ~EUR 1 billion funding requirement of both projects.

4.3 Capital Circulation Closed-Loop Model

Government Perspective — Capital Cycle

  1. Raising side: Indonesia's MoF issues Panda Bonds in China's interbank market, obtaining low-cost RMB funding at 1.90%–2.19% with an AAA rating
  2. Allocation side: Funds channeled into the two strategic projects via on-lending/investment channels
  3. Return side:
    • Nickel project: $95M annual profit → on-lending principal & interest repayment + taxes
    • Gas project: $3.16B contractor FCF → equity dividends / exit gains + taxes
  4. Strategic side: Government gains control over strategic nickel resources (core of downstreaming) + natural gas energy security + employment/FX/tax revenue
  5. Closed loop: Project returns cover Panda Bond principal and interest service — creating a sustainable capital cycle

5.3 Key Feasibility Judgments

Conclusion: The proposal is practically feasible, but requires strong government-relations advocacy

1. Financing instrument feasibility (HIGH): Panda Bond re-issuance is technically, legally and commercially validated. A RMB 8–10 billion re-issuance is a moderate increment over the RMB 7 billion inaugural issue — well within market capacity.

2. Funding channel feasibility (MEDIUM-HIGH): All three channels have legal basis and precedent. On-lending via state-owned banks is the most mature; Danantara investment best fits strategic needs. The key is getting both projects onto the government's priority project list.

3. Project quality feasibility (HIGH): Nickel project ROI 95% with 11-month payback; gas project NPV USD 789M — both projects' financial returns far exceed funding costs, with ample debt service capacity.

4. Political feasibility (MEDIUM): Projects align with Indonesia's national strategy (downstreaming + energy security), but require the intermediary to effectively secure senior government endorsement and persuade the Ministry of Finance to incorporate them into the Panda Bond re-issuance use-of-proceeds framework.

5. Timing feasibility (MEDIUM-HIGH): The Finance Minister has signaled re-issuance "within months," and the 2027 budget is under parliamentary review — now is the optimal window to push for project inclusion.

8Core Recommendations & Conclusion

Overall Conclusion: The proposal is feasible and warrants full pursuit

The core logic — "Indonesia's government re-issues Panda Bonds to raise low-cost RMB funding, channeled through compliant mechanisms into high-return strategic projects" — is feasible across all four dimensions: legal framework, market conditions, project quality and strategic alignment. The critical success factor is whether the intermediary can effectively secure senior Indonesian government endorsement to incorporate both projects into the Panda Bond re-issuance use-of-proceeds framework.

Core Recommendations for the Intermediary

  1. Position this as a "strategic resource development financing solution", not a simple corporate financing request. Emphasize to the government: this is not about finding money for two companies — it is about using the innovative Panda Bond instrument to give the Indonesian government control over strategic nickel and gas resources while earning spread income.
  2. Prioritize Danantara engagement. Danantara is the sovereign investment vehicle personally championed by President Prabowo, positioned as "the bridge between the national budget and private investment," with existing nickel/energy investment experience. The Danantara route is faster and more flexible than seeking dedicated budget allocations.
  3. Leverage the inaugural Panda Bond success narrative. RMB 7 billion raised, 2.4x oversubscribed, AAA rated — this is the Finance Ministry's achievement. Frame the re-issuance as "consolidating the Panda Bond success and scaling it up," with the two projects as the strategic use of proceeds.
  4. Timing is critical. The 2027 APBN budget is under parliamentary review (the President submitted the draft to Parliament on August 14, 2026) — this is the optimal window for project inclusion. The Finance Minister has also signaled re-issuance "within months" — the preparation window is now.
  5. The Chinese enterprise side can engage government directly. The enterprises can present project value directly to the Indonesian government / BUMN / Danantara, forming a dual-track approach alongside the intermediary's government coordination.
  6. Prepare a Plan B. If the direct government channel progresses slowly, alternatives include: China Eximbank / China Development Bank project financing, arrangements under the China-Indonesia bilateral currency swap agreement, or an AIIB multilateral guarantee structure.
